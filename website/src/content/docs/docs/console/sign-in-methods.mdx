The project's **Sign in methods** page starts with **MFA and passkeys**, then lists the four providers: Google, Apple, GitHub, and Microsoft. Each card says whether it is **On** or **Off**, and which ways of signing in are ready: **Redirect** (the browser goes to the provider) and **Native** (Google and Apple in a mobile app). Owners and developers change them; viewers see everything, with the fields turned off.

## MFA and passkeys

Two cards sit above the providers.

- **Authenticator app** turns [MFA](/docs/auth/mfa/) on or off for the whole project with its **Enabled** switch. It is on by default. Turning it off stops asking anyone for a code, and keeps every user's app and recovery codes for when you turn it back on.
- **Passkeys** opens a dialog with the **Enabled** switch, the **RP ID** (the domain passkeys belong to, such as `example.com`), the **RP name** (the name a passkey prompt shows; empty means the project's name), and the **Android certificate fingerprints** of your Android apps, one per line. Below them it lists the origins a passkey is accepted from today, and, for a project with iOS or Android [platforms](/docs/console/platforms/), the `apple-app-site-association` and `assetlinks.json` files to serve on your domain, each with a copy button. [Passkeys](/docs/auth/passkeys/) walks through the setup.

Changing the RP ID while passkeys can sign in shows how many will stop working, and asks you to type the new RP ID again to confirm. Those passkeys come back if you change it back.

## Set up a provider

1. Select **Set up** (or **Edit**) on the provider's card.
2. Copy the **Callback URL** with its copy button, and register it at the provider. Each provider's guide says where: [Google](/docs/auth/sign-in-with-google/), [Apple](/docs/auth/sign-in-with-apple/), [GitHub](/docs/auth/sign-in-with-github/), [Microsoft](/docs/auth/sign-in-with-microsoft/).
3. Fill in the IDs and the secret the provider gave you.
4. Turn on **Enabled** and select **Save**. A provider can be on only once one way of signing in is ready.

A field the server refuses shows its message under that field.

## Secrets

Client secrets and Apple's private key are stored encrypted and never shown again. A set secret shows as **Set, ends in** and its last 4 characters. **Replace** lets you type a new one; **Clear** removes it when you save; leaving it alone keeps it.

## On the Users pages

The Users list has a **Sign in** column: a mark for each linked provider, then **Password** for a user with a password, or **Email** for a user with neither. Its **MFA** column says **On** or **Off**, and the **MFA** filter (All, On, Off) finds either group. A user's page lists their **Identities** (the provider, the email at the provider, and when it was linked and last used) with **Unlink**. Unlinking a user's only way in is refused: "This is the user's only way to sign in." The sessions table names the provider beside the method, the session's **Level** (one factor or two), and its **Factors**.

A user's **Security** section shows whether MFA is on, when it was turned on, and how many recovery codes are left, then the user's passkeys: their name, when they were added and last used, whether they are synced or device bound, and whether they can sign in now. When a user lost every factor, **Reset MFA** removes their authenticator app and recovery codes and ends all their sessions; their passkeys stay. **Remove** deletes one passkey. Both ask you to confirm, and both email the user an alert. Viewers see the section with the buttons turned off.
